# Ferrous vs. Non-Ferrous Casting: Key Differences and Applications

Metal casting is widely used in industrial manufacturing to produce components in different shapes, sizes, and material grades. Understanding the differences between ferrous and non-ferrous casting helps manufacturers select suitable materials for their applications.

## What Is Ferrous Casting?

Ferrous casting involves metals that contain iron, such as carbon steel, alloy steel, stainless steel, and cast iron. These materials are often used for components that require strength, durability, and reliable mechanical performance.

## What Is Non-Ferrous Casting?

Non-ferrous casting uses metals that do not have iron as their main base metal. Common examples include aluminum, copper, brass, and magnesium alloys. Depending on the material, these metals can provide benefits such as reduced weight and specific corrosion-resistant properties.

## Ferrous vs. Non-Ferrous Casting

| Feature | Ferrous Casting | Non-Ferrous Casting |
|---|---|---|
| Main composition | Iron-based metals | Metals not primarily based on iron |
| Examples | Carbon steel, stainless steel, cast iron | Aluminum, copper, brass |
| Common advantages | Strength and durability | Potentially lower weight and specialized properties |
| Material selection | Depends on strength and operating requirements | Depends on weight, environment, and required properties |

## Industrial Applications

Ferrous and non-ferrous castings are used across different manufacturing sectors. The appropriate material depends on the component's design, operating conditions, mechanical requirements, and budget.
